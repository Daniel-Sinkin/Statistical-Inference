# Problem
Use Theorem 2.1.8 to find the pdf of $Y$ in Example 2.1.2. Show that the same answer is obtained by differentiating the cdf given in (2.1.6).

# Solution
We have
$$
X \sim U(0, 2\pi), Y = \sin^2(X)
$$
and with that $Y = g(X)$ where
$$
g(x) = \sin^2(x).
$$

It can be easily seen that $g(x)=\sin^2 x$ is monotone increasing on $\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\pi,\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)$, and monotone decreasing on $\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)$ and $\left(\frac{3\pi}{2},2\pi\right)$.

The preimage on each of those slices is $(0, 1)$, so the same, g is diffeomorphic on each of the restrictions, so in particular each slice has continuous derivative, as such we can apply Theorem 2.1.8 to get the representation.
On each monotone slice, the inverse mapping exists and is differentiable, and the image of each slice is $(0,1)$. Since $X$ is uniform on $(0,2\pi)$, its density is constant on each slice.

On each monotone slice, the inverse mapping exists and is differentiable, and the image of each slice is $(0,1)$. Since $X$ is uniform on $(0,2\pi)$, its density is constant on each slice.

Therefore, by Theorem 2.1.8, the density of $Y$ is obtained by summing the contributions of all inverse branches over these slices. Each branch contributes a term of the form
$$
f_X(g_i^{-1}(y)) \left| \frac{d}{dy} g_i^{-1}(y) \right|.
$$
Since $X \sim U(0,2\pi)$, we have
$$
f_X(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}, \qquad 0<x<2\pi,
$$
and $f_X(x)=0$ otherwise. Hence, for $y \in (0,1)$ (so that each $g_i^{-1}(y)$ lies in $(0,2\pi)$),
$$
f_X(g_i^{-1}(y))=\frac{1}{2\pi}.
$$

The CDF in (2.1.6) is given by
$$
P(Y \leq y) = 2P(X \leq x_1) + 2P(x_2 \leq X \leq \pi),
$$
where $x_1 < x_2$ are the two solutions of
$$
\sin^2(y) = y
$$
for $x \in (0, \pi)$. For $y \in (0, 1)$ it can be written as
$$
F_Y(y)=P(Y\le y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(\sqrt y),
$$
and $F_Y(y)=0$ for $y\le 0$, $F_Y(y)=1$ for $y\ge 1$. This is differentiable and we get
$$
f_Y(y)=F_Y'(y)
=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot \frac{d}{dy}\arcsin(\sqrt y)
=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(\sqrt y)^2}}\cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt y}
=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{y(1-y)}}.
$$
Hence
$$
f_Y(y)=
\begin{cases}
\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{y(1-y)}}, & 0<y<1,\\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
